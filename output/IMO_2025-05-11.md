## Capex Guidance

- **Prior Quarter:** 2024 capex maintained at $1.7B as guided. Management characterized 2025 planning as evaluating a "modest step-up" — directionally sub-20% above the current-year level — tied to Kearl reliability and Cold Lake growth, but deferred formal guidance to the February cycle. Language was explicitly provisional: "we want to work through the budget process before committing to a number."
- **Current Quarter:** 2025 capex formally set at $2.1B, a 24% increase from 2024. Allocation is specific: ~$800M to Kearl reliability and debottlenecking, ~$600M to Cold Lake (predominantly Grand Rapids Phase 2), balance to sustaining and downstream. Management anchored every line item to $70 WTI economics and emphasized the program is fully funded by free cash flow without balance sheet stretch.

**Signal: Bullish** — The move from vague directional language to a fully specified $2.1B budget, with explicit project-level IRR thresholds and free cash flow coverage disclosed in the same breath, signals that management completed its internal underwriting process with conviction; the 24% capex step-up paired with a production guidance raise and dividend increase is a coordinated statement of confidence, not a cost-push.

## Production / Volume Outlook

- **Prior Quarter:** Full-year 2024 guidance maintained at 420–430 kboe/d, though management flagged that a Q3 compressor outage at Kearl required Q4 to carry extra weight to hit the top end. CFO guided Kearl operating costs of $22–$23/bbl for 2025 with no structural improvement below $22 expected. No formal 2025 volume guidance issued.
- **Current Quarter:** 2024 delivered at 428 kboe/d, top of the range and a stated company record. 2025 guidance set at 430–440 kboe/d (midpoint 435, described as a new record). Quarterly phasing disclosed: Q1 modelled at 415–420k gross at Kearl, with acceleration through Q2–Q3 as debottlenecking comes online. Management flagged a Q4 2025 exit rate "north of 450,000 bpd at Kearl if everything executes."

**Signal: Bullish** — The upgrade from a maintained 420–430 range to a 430–440 guide — with a credible exit-rate optionality above 450k — accompanied by an explanation of the specific operational catalysts (secondary extraction circuit, Grand Rapids Phase 2 ramp) makes this more than a stretch target; investors should track Q2 production as the first real test of the debottlenecking thesis.

## Buyback / Dividend Policy

- **Prior Quarter:** NCIB renewal described as pending board evaluation, with no decision announced. CEO stated "no reason not to renew" but characterized the timing as following "normal process." 2024 combined shareholder returns running at ~$520M per quarter (NCIB + dividends). Dividend growth trajectory expected to continue but no specific increase signalled.
- **Current Quarter:** NCIB formally renewed and extended 12 months, authorized at 5% of shares outstanding. 2024 full-year shareholder returns disclosed at $1.4B ($1.1B buyback, $280M dividends). 2025 total return target set at "at least $1.5B" at current strip pricing. Quarterly dividend increased 13% to $0.44/share effective March, marking the 29th consecutive year of dividend growth.

**Signal: Bullish** — Converting the tentative NCIB language from Q3 into a confirmed renewal plus a 13% dividend increase in a single call removes the optionality discount the market had been pricing; the 29-year dividend growth streak disclosure and explicit $1.5B+ 2025 return target function as a floor commitment, not an aspiration.

## Hedging Posture

- **Prior Quarter:** No crude price hedges in place. Policy described as "substantially unhedged" to provide shareholders full commodity upside. Modest CAD/USD financial hedging only. Management characterized the posture as unchanged and not anticipated to change.
- **Current Quarter:** Policy described as unchanged in principle, but a tactical exception disclosed: ~20,000 bpd of WCS-WTI differential hedges placed in late Q4 at minus $14.50/bbl for Q1–Q2 2025. Management explicitly framed this as a response to U.S. tariff policy uncertainty on Canadian crude exports, not a view change on commodity prices. No intention to expand the hedge book beyond this position.

**Signal: Watch** — The first disclosed deviation from a long-standing no-hedge policy, even if tactically framed and modest in scale (~5% of upstream volumes), is worth flagging: it signals that management's private risk assessment of the tariff scenario is material enough to warrant balance sheet protection, which is directionally more cautious than Q3 language regardless of the public framing.

## New Risk Factors

- **Prior Quarter:** TMX differential benefit characterized as positive but durability uncertain ("cautiously optimistic but not assuming a step-change"). U.S. tariff policy noted as a monitoring item with "limited direct effects" given integrated downstream position. No formal scenario analysis disclosed.
- **Current Quarter:** U.S. tariff risk on Canadian crude exports elevated from a monitoring item to an explicit material uncertainty with scenario analysis disclosed. CFO quantified a $5/bbl WCS differential widening at ~$250–300M pre-tax free cash flow impact annually, with a 30–40 cent integrated downstream offset acknowledged. CEO stated the risk "didn't feature in our planning last year and warrants disclosure" — a direct acknowledgment that the risk factor is new to the formal disclosure framework. Grand Rapids Phase 2 upside risk (recovery factors tracking 15% above model) added as a positive optionality factor not mentioned in Q3.

**Signal: Watch** — The tariff risk has been upgraded from boilerplate monitoring language to a quantified scenario with a partial hedge overlay; the FCF sensitivity math ($250–300M pre-tax before integration offset, on a ~$2.3B FCF base) is manageable at current strip but a 10% tariff sustained through 2025 would meaningfully pressure the $1.5B shareholder return target — investors should monitor U.S. trade policy developments as the primary binary for the stock over the next two quarters.
